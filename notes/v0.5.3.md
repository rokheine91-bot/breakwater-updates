# Tsunami Defense 0.5.3

Water 1.2: a new sea solver. Same 5 cm cells. The old solver could only do the basics; the new one is the standard method for flooding and dam-break simulations.

- The sea is computed as volumes of water passing between cells. No water is created or lost, and still water stays perfectly still over any sea bed, including islands and dry banks.
- Sharper wave fronts and bores without wobble. Water runs up onto dry land and drains back cleanly.
- Water squeezing between the house walls now shoots out as jets on the far side.
- Sandy-bottom friction, so run-up and backwash slow down realistically.
- Before each frame the graphics card decides how many substeps it needs: 8 in calm water, up to 16 when water runs fast. It costs about 3 to 6.5 ms on a GTX 1080 Ti.
- Checks:
  - wave height and speed within 4 %
  - run-up within 1 to 8 % of the lab law for wave height to depth 0.05 to 0.20 (was -16 % at 0.15)
  - dam break within 1 to 4 % of the exact solution
  - a lake over a bumpy bed stays still
  - volume holds in a closed tank
- Tests now wait for the graphics card every frame, so they repeat bit for bit. Normal play does not wait: it is about 20 % faster, but two runs can differ by tiny rounding amounts. Start the game with `--exact` to get bit-identical runs.
