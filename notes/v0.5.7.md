# Tsunami Defense 0.5.7

Water 1.5: the bricks feel the 3D water.

- Inside the 3D zone the house is now pushed by the 3D water instead of by the sea:
  - Each open brick face takes the pressure of the water cell next to it. That pressure includes both still water and the push of water that is stopped by the wall.
  - Loose bricks in the water get drag and buoyancy from the 3D flow.
- The house now breaks from the real 3D load. It starts breaking earlier, when the first big wave hits (about 140 pieces at 22 s, where before it held), and ends with about 770 pieces loose instead of about 490.
- The 3D water no longer sinks: particles that bunch up are spread out again. At the peak of the wave the zone's water now stands within about 0.1 to 0.5 m of the sea's (was 0.1 to 0.8 m).
- Check: the 3D pressure in still water matches the textbook rho g depth within 13 % (target 15 %). Most of the difference comes from where inside the top 15 cm cell the surface sits.
- Not yet:
  - Loose bricks do not yet block the 3D water. It flows through them and only pushes them along.
  - The check against an impact formula for a breaking wave on a wall is not done yet.
  - The zone still does not hand its water back to the sea. It now could, but that makes spray pile up at the zone edge and costs frames, so it stays off for the benchmarks phase.
- Speed is unchanged: about 49 to 55 fps during the impact on a GTX 1080 Ti (target 60, for the benchmarks phase).
