# Tsunami Defense 0.5.11

Water 1.9: the open points from the water review.

- Breaking wave on a wall, checked against engineering formulas (test tank, a 1 m dam break hitting a wall 2 m away):
  - the steady push on the wall while the water piles up (2.5 to 2.9 kN per metre of wall) matches the momentum of the incoming water (2.9 kN/m) within 15 %
  - the FEMA P-646 surge force (4.4 kN/m) stays above it, as a design upper bound should
  - the first slam is a short spike of 8.3 kN/m, and the pressure at the foot of the wall peaks at 2.3 times the still-water pressure (lab experiments: about 3 times)
  - the 3D pressure is noisy: some frames read near zero. The bricks average over many substeps, so this is not visible in play.
- Loose bricks now block the 3D water. A cell that is at least a quarter full of loose brick becomes a moving solid that carries the bricks' speed, so the water flows around rubble and pushes it, instead of passing through it.
- The 3D zone now feeds its water back into the sea:
  - The sea follows the zone's water surface, gently and fading in over the first 6 m inside the zone's edge.
  - A wave passing into the zone throws back 3.7 % of its height. The first attempt threw back 70 %.
  - Inside the zone the sea no longer makes spray: the zone's own 3D water is the splash there. This removes the spray that piled up along the zone's edge.
- Pouring drops blobs of water, as in 2D:
  - A click drops a ball of water 0.6 m across from 2 m up. Holding the button drops another every quarter second.
  - Outside the 3D zone the blob is 2000 drops that join the sea where they land, and the sea plus the drops gain exactly the blob's water.
  - Inside the zone it is 3D water, within 8 % of the blob's volume on 15 cm cells.
  - These sizes are my defaults; say if you want them bigger, smaller or higher.
- The mouse now aims at the ground as you have raised or lowered it with the brush.
- 1 m waves and spray. A 1 m wave makes:
  - no spray on the rock coast: the steep shore makes the wave surge up instead of breaking, which is what real steep shores do
  - some spray on the long gentle shelf: about 4,800 drops, and 37,000 drops with the house there
  - plenty of spray at 2 m and above
  - The 5 cm sea cells are fine for this, so the spray rules were left as they are.
- Benchmarks on a GTX 1080 Ti, whole level program (the biggest wave is 3 m):

- All earlier water checks still pass: lake still, dam break, run-up, Green's law, total water, exact spray exchange, tools, ground brush.
- Known issue: long runs with the house are not bit-identical. After about 25 to 40 s the bricks' positions differ from run to run, in 0.5.10 too. With feedback on, the difference now also reaches the water. Short runs and the water-only tests are still identical every time.
