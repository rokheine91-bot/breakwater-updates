# Tsunami Defense 0.5.6

Water 1.4, part 2: the 3D impact zone is in the game.

- A box of fully 3D water, 20 x 24 m, goes around the house. It starts 9 m on the sea side, covers the full width and reaches 6 m above the sea. It uses 15 cm cells, with particles only in the top 3 cells of the water.
- The sea feeds the zone at its sea-side edge: the sea's surface and flow come in, and water flowing back out returns to the sea. Inside the zone the water curls, splashes up the walls, shoots between and around them, and runs over the land in 3D. The house's standing walls are solid in the zone; as they break, the water flows through the gaps.
- Z turns the zone on and off. Without it, the game plays exactly as in 0.5.4.
- For now the sea still pushes the house. The zone shows the 3D water but does not hand its water back to the sea, because its water stands 0.1 to 0.8 m lower than the sea's at the peak and the house would break less. The 3D water's own pressure on the bricks comes in water 1.5.
- Spray drops and the zone's particles are now drawn as small squares facing the camera instead of cubes. They look about the same and draw 6 times fewer corners.
- The zone runs identically every time when the game is started with `--exact`. Making the zone repeatable meant fixing how the graphics card reads the particle counts it writes itself.
- Speed on a GTX 1080 Ti: the zone costs 2 to 5.5 ms. During the impact the whole frame runs at about 47 to 54 fps, below the 60 fps target; this gets fixed in the benchmarks phase.
- Not yet:
  - the sea's own repeatability test still gives a different result now and then (1.8)
  - the 3D water pressing on bricks (1.5)
  - a proper water surface for the zone (1.6)
