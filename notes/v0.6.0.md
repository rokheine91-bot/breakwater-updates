# Tsunami Defense 0.6.0

Water 2.0: the water looks like water.

- Realistic water surface:
  - You see the sand and rocks through the water, bent by the waves, fading to blue-green the deeper you look.
  - The sky is reflected, strongest when you look across the water at a low angle.
  - Fine ripples move over the surface; foam forms where water runs fast or thin or a wave breaks.
  - The flat colour bands of the old pixel look are gone.
- No more little squares:
  - Water from the 3D zone is drawn as particles only where it really flies through the air (splashes, jets). Everything else is one smooth surface.
  - Water running over the land is drawn from the 5 cm sea surface, so it reads as a thin sheet, not as loose drops.
- Whitewater instead of confetti:
  - Spray is thrown as fewer, bigger blobs (150 ml instead of 50 ml): 6,700 drops for a 2 m wave on the rock coast instead of 135,000.
  - Spray and splashes are white and soft, never dark grey. Foam lies on the water as soft patches.
- Breaking fronts:
  - Where a wave front is steep and moving, its face leans forward and foams, instead of standing as an upright wall.
  - The wave now arrives slightly differently along the shore (up to 18 % lower in places, up to 1.5 m earlier or later), and the sea floor in front of the beach has gentle bumps. The front bends instead of arriving as a ruler-straight line. The biggest wave of a level is never higher than designed.
  - The water tests still use a straight wave and a flat sea floor, so they still compare with the lab results.
- Pouring: each blob is now 250 whitewater blobs instead of 2,000 drops.
- Benchmarks on a GTX 1080 Ti, whole level program (biggest wave 3 m). Visuals came first this round; speed is the next phase.

- All water checks still pass: lake still, dam break, run-up, Green's law, total water, exact spray exchange, tools, ground brush, blob, zone border reflection 3.7 %, repeatable runs.
- Not done this round:
  - Moving thin water fully from the 3D zone to the sea (the physics handover). It risks doubling or losing water at the wave front without a proper exchange of water at a moving border. The look it was meant to fix is fixed by drawing thin water from the sea.
  - Some grey, rounded shapes can show along a breaking front where the leaning surface folds over the whitewater.
